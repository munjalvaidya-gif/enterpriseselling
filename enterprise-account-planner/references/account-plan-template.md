# Account Plan Template

Fill in every section below for the finished plan. Keep the headers and order exactly as written — this is the fixed shape of every plan this skill produces. Where a fact isn't available, write "No public data found" rather than leaving a blank or guessing.

```markdown
# Account Plan: {Company Name}

**Vertical:** {vertical}
**Seller platform:** {what the seller sells}
**Prepared:** {date}
**Research mode:** {Live research / Template — fill in yourself}

---

## 1. Customer Information

- **Overview:** {2-4 sentences on what the company does and how it makes money}
- **Headquarters:** {city, country}
- **Employees:** {approximate count, with source/date}
- **Annual revenue / market cap:** {figure, with source/date}
- **Ownership:** {public (ticker) / private / PE-backed / subsidiary of ...}
- **Key facts:** {any other detail a rep should walk in knowing — major brands, recent milestones, public reputation}

## 2. Funding & Financial Position

- **If private/PE-backed:** funding rounds in a table — Round | Amount | Date | Lead investor(s)
- **If public:** recent earnings highlights, stock trajectory over the last 12 months, notable M&A activity, analyst sentiment
- **Financial signals relevant to a purchase decision:** {cost-cutting mode? growth mode? recent layoffs or hiring surge? budget season timing?}

## 3. Organization Structure & Recent Changes

- **Leadership relevant to this deal:** {names/titles of executives who'd plausibly own or influence a purchase in this category}
- **Recent changes:** {reorgs, layoffs, new hires, departures — with dates and sources}
- **Reporting structure:** {who reports to whom, where relevant — e.g. does the CISO report to the CIO or directly to the board?}

## 4. Decision-Maker Signals

| Name | Title | Signal | Source | Why it matters |
|------|-------|--------|--------|-----------------|
| {name} | {title} | {e.g. "posted about AI cost overruns on LinkedIn, June 2026"} | {link/publication} | {what this tells the rep} |

Include at least the likely economic buyer and one or two influencers/champions if identifiable. If names can't be confirmed, list the likely titles/functions instead and mark them as unconfirmed.

## 5. Tech Stack

- **Currently in use (confirmed or strongly signaled):** {tool — source, e.g. job posting mentioning it}
- **Adjacent/competitive to what we sell:** {call out anything that competes with or overlaps the seller's platform}
- **Whitespace / gaps:** {what's plausibly missing given their stack and stage — this is often the opening}

## 6. Entry Points & Persona to Pitch

- **Primary persona to target:** {title/function, based on the platform-persona playbook}
- **Likely champion profile:** {who inside the org would advocate for this internally}
- **Entry point / trigger event:** {the specific reason "now" is a plausible time to reach out — a hire, a reorg, an earnings statement, a public initiative}
- **Recommended opening angle:** {1-2 sentences on how to frame the first conversation, tied to the trigger}

## 7. Account Penetration & Expansion Strategy

- **Initial wedge (penetration):** {the smallest, lowest-friction entry point to land first — a specific team, use case, or budget line, not "the whole company"}
- **Land motion:** {how the first deal likely gets structured — pilot, single-team rollout, proof of concept — tied to Section 6's entry point}
- **Expansion path:** {once landed, where this plausibly grows next — adjacent teams, additional use cases, upsell/cross-sell surface — tied to the org structure from Section 3}
- **Expansion triggers:** {the internal signal that would indicate it's time to push expansion — e.g. a renewal window, a budget cycle, an org change}

## 8. Key Events & Industry Touchpoints

- **Events where this account's stakeholders are likely present:** {named industry conferences, trade shows, earnings calls/investor days, or vertical-specific summits if findable; otherwise the *type* of event typical for this vertical (e.g. "regional CIO summits," "[vertical] trade association annual conference") rather than leaving this blank}
- **Why these matter:** {a shared conference, panel, or booth conversation is a warmer entry point than cold outreach — say specifically how}
- **Timing:** {any known dates or cadence — annual conference month, earnings call schedule}

## 9. Discovery Question Guide (First Stakeholder Meeting)

Pull 6-8 questions from `references/discovery-question-bank.md`, prioritizing whichever MEDDPICC elements are marked "inferred" or "No public data found" below — those are the actual gaps worth spending meeting time on. Organize by who's in the room:

**For the Economic Buyer ({persona from Section 6}):**
- {question} — closes: {MEDDPICC element}
- {question} — closes: {MEDDPICC element}

**For the Champion ({persona from Section 6}):**
- {question} — closes: {MEDDPICC element}
- {question} — closes: {MEDDPICC element}

---

## MEDDPICC Snapshot

- **Metrics:** {what business outcome/number would this purchase need to move}
- **Economic Buyer:** {who signs off — name/title if known}
- **Decision Criteria:** {what this org likely evaluates vendors on, based on their stack/vertical/stated priorities}
- **Decision Process:** {likely procurement path — best guess based on company size/structure, marked as inferred}
- **Paper Process:** {likely legal/security review complexity — e.g. public company + regulated vertical = heavier}
- **Identify Pain:** {the business pain this platform would plausibly solve for this account, tied to Section 5/6 findings}
- **Champion:** {same as Section 6's champion profile, restated briefly}
- **Competition:** {incumbent vendors or likely competitive alternatives, from Section 5}

---

## Sources

{list every source used, as a simple list of links/publications with dates}
```
