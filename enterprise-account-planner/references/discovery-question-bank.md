# Discovery Question Bank

A ready-to-use set of questions for the seller's first real meeting with account stakeholders — the moment research hands off to conversation. Organized by persona, since a real meeting is with one specific person, not an abstract MEDDPICC checklist. Each question is tagged with the MEDDPICC element it's designed to surface.

## How to build the actual list for a meeting

Don't hand the seller all of these — that's a checklist, not a conversation, and it'll read as canned. Build a specific 6-8 question list per meeting:

1. **Start from the gaps.** Look at the plan's MEDDPICC Snapshot and find every element marked "inferred" or "No public data found." Those are the things research couldn't answer — which makes them exactly what's worth spending face time on.
2. **Match gaps to persona.** Pull the questions below for whichever persona is in the room (Economic Buyer questions if it's the exec, Champion questions if it's the practitioner — most first meetings are with one or the other, not both).
3. **Add one opener tied to the actual trigger event.** Reference the specific thing research surfaced — a reorg, an earnings statement, a public initiative — rather than opening generically. This is what separates a plan-informed conversation from a cold-call script.

Keep the final list to 6-8 questions. Sequence from context → pain → process → criteria, and let the conversation breathe between them.

## Questions for the Economic Buyer

The person with budget authority — usually a VP or C-level, per Section 6's persona mapping.

- "What's the business outcome you're most accountable for this year that this kind of initiative would move?" — **[Metrics]**
- "If we were sitting here in 12 months and this was a clear win, what changed?" — **[Metrics]**
- "Walk me through how a decision like this typically gets made here — who else needs to be in the room?" — **[Decision Process]**
- "What would need to be true for this to be a top-3 priority for your budget this cycle?" — **[Decision Criteria]**
- "What's already in place today that this would need to work alongside or replace?" — **[Competition]**
- "Who on your team would end up owning this day to day?" — **[Champion]**
- "What would legal, procurement, or security review typically look like for something like this?" — **[Paper Process]**
- "What's the cost of not solving this problem in the next 12 months?" — **[Identify Pain]**

## Questions for the Champion / Practitioner

The hands-on person who'd advocate internally — usually a manager or senior individual contributor, per Section 6's champion profile.

- "What's the most painful part of your day-to-day that this could take off your plate?" — **[Identify Pain]**
- "What are you currently stitching together to work around this gap?" — **[Tech Stack / Competition]**
- "Has your team evaluated anything like this before? What happened?" — **[Competition / Decision Process]**
- "Who besides you would need to be convinced for this to move forward?" — **[Economic Buyer / Decision Process]**
- "What would make this an easy yes vs. a hard no for your leadership?" — **[Decision Criteria]**
- "What does success look like for you personally if this goes well?" — **[Champion motivation]**
- "Is there a budget cycle or planning moment coming up we should be aware of?" — **[Decision Process / timing]**

## Opener questions (either persona)

Use one of these to ground the conversation in something real before moving into the persona-specific list above:

- "I saw {the specific trigger event from Section 6} — how has that changed priorities on your end?"
- "What's changed in the last couple of quarters that makes this a different conversation than it would've been a year ago?"
- "What's the reaction internally been to {a specific public statement, earnings comment, or initiative found in research}?"

These only work if they're specific — a generic version of these questions ("so, what's new?") defeats the point of having done the research at all.
