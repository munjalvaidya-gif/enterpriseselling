# MEDDPICC Primer

MEDDPICC (the extended form of MEDDIC used in Force Management's methodology) is eight questions a rep must be able to answer before they trust a deal is real. Each element below maps to specific parts of the account plan research — use this to know what you're actually looking for, not just to fill in a label.

| Letter | Stands for | What you're really asking | Where it comes from in the plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M** | Metrics | What quantifiable business outcome does this purchase need to move (revenue, cost, risk, time)? | Inferred from Section 1 (business model), Section 2 (financial pressure), and Section 5 (whitespace) |
| **E** | Economic Buyer | Who has the actual authority and budget to say yes — not just an enthusiastic user? | Section 3 (leadership) + Section 4 (decision-maker signals) |
| **D** | Decision Criteria | What will this org actually evaluate vendors on — price, integration, compliance, brand risk? | Inferred from vertical (regulatory context), current tech stack (Section 5), and any stated priorities found in research |
| **D** | Decision Process | What are the steps and approvals a purchase like this goes through at this org? | Best-effort inference from company size/structure/ownership (public companies and regulated verticals almost always mean more steps) — always mark this as inferred unless directly confirmed |
| **P** | Paper Process | What will legal/security/procurement review look like, and how long will it take? | Inferred from ownership type (public/regulated = heavier), vertical, and company size |
| **I** | Identify Pain | What specific, provable pain does this account have that the seller's platform addresses? | Cross-reference Section 5 (tech stack gaps) and Section 6 (trigger event) against what the seller's platform actually does |
| **C** | Champion | Who inside the org benefits personally from this deal succeeding and has influence to push it? | Section 6 — distinct from the Economic Buyer; usually a practitioner or manager-level advocate, not the signer |
| **C** | Competition | Who else is this account plausibly evaluating — incumbent vendors, status quo, or a direct competitor? | Section 5 (adjacent/competitive tools already in place) |

## How to use this while researching

Don't treat MEDDPICC as a checklist to fill in after the fact — let it guide *what you search for*. For example:
- Searching for the CFO's public statements isn't just "leadership research," it's answering **Economic Buyer**.
- Finding a job posting for a "Data Platform Engineer" isn't just "tech stack," it's evidence toward **Identify Pain** (they're actively investing in this problem area) and **Competition** (what they're likely building around).
- A recent reorg that consolidates data teams under a new Chief Data Officer is simultaneously an **Economic Buyer** signal and an **Entry Point** trigger.

## What "inferred" means here

Decision Process and Paper Process are almost never publicly documented — you're reasoning from company characteristics (size, ownership, vertical, regulatory exposure), not citing a source. Always label these two as inferred/best-estimate in the output. The seller is expected to confirm the real process with their champion once they're in the door — the plan's job is to set a realistic starting expectation, not to claim certainty it can't have.
