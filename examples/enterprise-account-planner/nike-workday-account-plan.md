# Account Plan: Nike, Inc.

**Vertical:** Apparel / Footwear Retail
**Seller platform:** Workday
**Prepared:** 2026-08-14

## 1. Customer Information

- **Overview:** Nike, Inc. designs, markets, and sells athletic footwear, apparel, equipment, and accessories globally under the Nike, Jordan, and Converse brands. It makes money through wholesale distribution to retail partners and direct-to-consumer sales (Nike Direct — owned stores and digital/NIKE.com), supplemented by licensing.
- **Headquarters:** Beaverton, Oregon, USA
- **Employees:** Approximately 73,000 worldwide, including retail and part-time staff, as of May 31, 2026 (source: NIKE, Inc. FY2026 Form 10-K)
- **Annual revenue / market cap:** $46.4 billion in revenue for fiscal year 2026 (flat reported, down 2% currency-neutral); FY2026 net income $3.11B vs. $3.22B prior year (source: NIKE, Inc. FY2026 Q4/full-year earnings release, June 30, 2026)
- **Ownership:** Public — NYSE: NKE
- **Key facts:** Nike is in year three of a publicly acknowledged corporate turnaround ("Win Now") under CEO Elliott Hill, who returned to lead the company after the John Donahoe era's DTC-heavy strategy underperformed. The company is mid-reorganization around a new "Sport Offense" operating model and is absorbing roughly $1.5B in annualized tariff-driven cost increases. Stock has fallen from a 2024 high above $100 to roughly $40-41 as of August 2026.

## 2. Funding & Financial Position

- **Public company financials:** FY2026 revenue $46.4B (flat/-2% FX-neutral); gross margin compressed ~300bps in Q2 FY2026 to 40.6%, driven by tariffs and Greater China inventory obsolescence. Stock closed at $40.51 on August 12, 2026 — down from $106.40 in February 2024, $75.86 at end of 2024, and $64.03 at end of 2025 (source: MacroTrends, StockAnalysis, Yahoo Finance historical pricing). Analyst average 12-month price target ~$50, implying modest expected recovery; JPMorgan downgraded to Underweight with a $40 target in 2026.
- **M&A activity:** No public data found on recent M&A. Note the CFO transition below is itself a governance signal worth tracking.
- **Financial signals relevant to a purchase decision:** Clearly in **cost-discipline mode**, not growth-spend mode. Three consecutive years of workforce reductions (2024: ~1,600 roles / 2% of workforce; August 2025: ~1% of corporate staff; January 2026: ~775 roles at Mississippi/Tennessee distribution centers; April 2026: ~1,400 roles concentrated in technology) plus a ~$1.5B annualized tariff cost headwind mean any new software purchase will be scrutinized hard on ROI and consolidation value, not innovation-for-its-own-sake. New CFO David Denton (formerly Pfizer CFO) starts August 2026, replacing Matt Friend — new finance leadership often triggers a fresh vendor/spend review in the first 2-3 quarters, which is a real timing signal for Section 6/7 below.

## 3. Organization Structure & Recent Changes

- **Leadership relevant to this deal:**
  - **Treasure Heinle** — EVP, Chief People Officer (Nike's CHRO-equivalent title), in role since January 6, 2025, promoted internally from Chief Talent Officer. She succeeded Monique Matheson, who retired after 26 years at Nike (source: NIKE, Inc. newsroom / BusinessWire, November 2024; about.nike.com executive bio).
  - **Elliott Hill** — President & CEO, driving the "Win Now" turnaround and "Sport Offense" reorganization.
  - **David Denton** (incoming, August 2026) — EVP & CFO, formerly CFO of Pfizer, replacing Matt Friend, who had his remit expanded to include Global Sales and Nike Direct shortly before his departure was announced (source: Sahm Capital, WWD, June 2026).
  - **Cheryan Jacob** — newly created Chief Information Officer role (hired from Microsoft/Flexport), overseeing enterprise architecture, platform engineering, security/risk/compliance, and vendor strategy — **vendor strategy and management sits explicitly in his remit**, which matters for how a Workday deal would get technically vetted (source: Consumer Goods Technology, BusinessOfFashion).
  - **Venky Alagirisamy** — promoted from Chief Supply Chain Officer to COO in December 2025, absorbing oversight of the technology organization after the CTO role was eliminated.
- **Recent changes:** December 2025 leadership consolidation eliminated the CTO role (Muge Dogan departed) and the Chief Commercial Officer role (Craig Williams departed), folding tech oversight into the new COO position. This is the third consecutive year of workforce reductions (2024, 2025, 2026 — detailed in Section 2). The "Sport Offense" operating model, announced in 2026, redeployed approximately **8,000 employees** into new vertical sport-category teams (running, basketball, training, global football, etc.), unifying product creation, innovation, and storytelling across Nike, Jordan, and Converse (source: Yahoo Finance/Nasdaq "Sport Offense" coverage, Forbes, July 2026).
- **Reporting structure:** Chief People Officer Heinle leads HR/People globally and reports to the CEO per standard EVP structure (about.nike.com). Technology now reports through the COO rather than a standalone CTO — meaning HR technology purchases likely require sign-off that touches both the CPO's organization and the COO/CIO's technology governance, not the CPO alone.

## 5. Tech Stack

- **Currently in use (confirmed or strongly signaled):** A Nike careers posting for a "Time & Attendance Business Analyst" role describes supporting implementation of **UKG Pro Workforce Management** for time & attendance, and separately states "general understanding of Workday Core HCM desired but not required" for the role (source: Nike careers listing via LiveHire/Humanforce, cross-referenced against ZipRecruiter listings). This is a genuine but **inferred, not confirmed** signal — the phrasing suggests Workday Core HCM may already be present somewhere in Nike's environment (a subsidiary, a specific region, or a partial rollout), with UKG layered on top for time/attendance specifically. This needs direct confirmation in discovery; do not repeat it as fact in a meeting.
- **Adjacent/competitive to what we sell:** UKG Pro Workforce Management (confirmed via job posting) directly competes with Workday's time-tracking/workforce management capability, even if it doesn't compete with full Core HCM. If the Workday Core HCM reference above is accurate, that reduces the size of the wedge opportunity (it becomes expansion, not net-new) — this is the single most important thing to verify early.
- **Whitespace / gaps:** No public data found confirming Nike's payroll, talent management, or financial planning HCM-adjacent stack. Given three consecutive years of restructuring and an 8,000-person internal redeployment under Sport Offense, workforce planning/analytics and org-design tooling is a plausible whitespace — manually managing that scale of redeployment without modern workforce planning software is a real operational strain, though this is inference, not a confirmed gap.

## 6. Entry Points & Persona to Pitch

- **Primary persona to target:** Chief People Officer (Nike's CHRO-equivalent) — **Treasure Heinle**, per the HR Tech / Workforce playbook category (Economic Buyer: CHRO, VP People, or CFO for payroll-adjacent tools). Given the CFO transition currently underway, CFO David Denton is a secondary/parallel economic-buyer path worth tracking once he's settled in, particularly if the deal touches payroll or headcount cost reporting.
- **Likely champion profile:** HRIS Manager or People Ops Lead within Heinle's organization — unconfirmed by name; the UKG Time & Attendance job posting (Section 5) confirms a practitioner-level People Technology function exists and is actively managing HR system implementations, which is where a champion would sit.
- **Entry point / trigger event:** The strongest, most specific trigger is the **compounding organizational complexity Nike is managing right now**: a brand-new CPO (14 months into role) inheriting a third consecutive year of layoffs, an 8,000-person Sport Offense redeployment across vertical sport categories, and a CTO-role elimination that pushed tech governance into a new COO/CIO structure — all while a new outside CFO arrives in August 2026. That combination (new HR leader + repeated restructuring + leadership churn) is exactly the pattern the playbook flags as a high-probability trigger for HR tech evaluation, because manual/legacy processes strain hardest during repeated reorganizations.
- **Recommended opening angle:** Lead with the operational reality of redeploying 8,000 employees into new Sport Offense category teams while simultaneously running a third straight year of workforce reductions — ask how Heinle's team is tracking and reporting on that scale of internal mobility today, rather than opening with a generic HCM pitch. This is specific to what Nike itself has publicly described, not a canned platform pitch.

## 7. Account Penetration & Expansion Strategy

- **Initial wedge (penetration):** Don't pitch a full Core HCM replacement as the opener — that invites a head-on fight with UKG (time/attendance) and a possible existing Workday footprint that hasn't been confirmed yet. The lower-friction wedge is **workforce planning and org-design/analytics** specifically scoped to the Sport Offense redeployment and the recurring restructuring cycle: a tool that lets the People org model, track, and report on large-scale internal mobility (8,000 people moved into new category teams) is a narrow, budget-defensible ask tied to a problem Nike has already gone on record about, rather than a company-wide platform swap.
- **Land motion:** A single-team pilot or proof of concept inside the Chief People Officer's organization, likely co-sponsored by whoever owns HR/People analytics reporting to Heinle — framed as directly supporting the next phase of Sport Offense execution rather than a generic "modernize HR tech" initiative. This keeps the deal small enough to move fast under a cost-conscious CFO transition, consistent with Section 6's entry point.
- **Expansion path:** If the workforce-planning wedge lands, the natural next surface is Core HCM/talent management (contingent on confirming whether Workday Core HCM already has a foothold per Section 5 — if it doesn't, this is the bigger prize; if it does, the expansion is deepening/consolidating that footprint instead). A second, CFO-adjacent expansion path opens once David Denton is settled: Workday's finance/planning capabilities could piggyback on the HR relationship, especially given his expanded predecessor's oversight of Sales/Direct suggests Nike leadership is already comfortable consolidating functions under fewer systems of record. Both paths tie back to the org structure changes documented in Section 3 (CPO, incoming CFO, COO absorbing tech).
- **Expansion triggers:** Watch for (1) Nike's Investor Day, planned for fall 2026, where new-CFO priorities and any stated technology/efficiency commitments would surface publicly; (2) the FY2027 budget cycle, which would follow shortly after Denton's arrival; (3) any renewal or contract event around the existing UKG relationship, which would be the natural moment to have the Core HCM/consolidation conversation if applicable.

## 9. Discovery Question Guide (First Stakeholder Meeting)

The MEDDPICC Snapshot below marks **Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, and Competition** as inferred or only partially confirmed — those are the real gaps, and the questions below are weighted toward closing them rather than re-asking things research already answered (e.g., don't waste time asking who the CEO is or whether there's been a reorg — we know that).

**For the Economic Buyer (Chief People Officer — Treasure Heinle or her direct delegate):**
- "I saw Nike redeployed roughly 8,000 people into the new Sport Offense category teams — how has your team been tracking and reporting on a move that size?" — closes: **Identify Pain** (this is the opener, grounded in the actual trigger event from Section 6, not a generic question)
- "Walk me through how a decision like this typically gets made here — who else needs to be in the room?" — closes: **Decision Process** (marked inferred in the snapshot — genuinely unknown)
- "What's already in place today that this would need to work alongside or replace?" — closes: **Competition** (directly targets the unconfirmed UKG/possible-Workday-Core-HCM overlap flagged in Section 5 — this is the single most important open question in the whole plan)
- "What would legal, procurement, or security review typically look like for something like this?" — closes: **Paper Process** (marked inferred — public company, so expect a real answer worth capturing)
- "With a new CFO starting in August, what would need to be true for a People systems investment to be a top-3 priority for budget this cycle?" — closes: **Decision Criteria** (ties directly to the Denton transition documented in Section 2/3 rather than asking generically)
- "What's the cost of not solving this the next time the organization restructures?" — closes: **Metrics** (three restructurings in three years makes "next time" a real, not hypothetical, frame)

**For the Champion (HRIS Manager / People Ops Lead — name unconfirmed):**
- "What are you currently stitching together to work around gaps in workforce planning during a reorg like Sport Offense?" — closes: **Tech Stack / Competition** (again targeting the UKG/Workday overlap question — this needs to be asked from both sides of the table)
- "Has the team evaluated anything like this before, and what happened?" — closes: **Competition / Decision Process** (useful cross-check against whatever the CPO says in the parallel conversation)
- "Who besides you would need to be convinced for this to move forward?" — closes: **Economic Buyer / Decision Process** (helps confirm whether Heinle alone signs, or whether CIO Cheryan Jacob's "vendor strategy and management" remit — Section 3 — means this is really a joint sign-off)
