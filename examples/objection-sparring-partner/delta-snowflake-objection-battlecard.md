# Objection Battlecard — Delta Air Lines

**Selling:** Snowflake
**Persona:** VP of Data & Analytics Engineering — economic buyer, skeptical after a prior data platform initiative (two years ago) went over budget and under-delivered
**Deal stage:** Evaluation
**Prepared:** 2026-08-17

## Deal snapshot

- **Account:** Delta Air Lines, airline vertical, public company. Seller has had two discovery calls with a Senior Data Engineering Manager who's enthusiastic about Snowflake but hasn't yet tested internal political capital to push it upward.
- **MEDDPICC state going in:**
  - Economic Buyer: confirmed — VP of Data & Analytics Engineering, but not yet spoken to directly.
  - Champion: assumed — Senior Data Engineering Manager is enthusiastic but untested as an internal advocate; hasn't been asked to go to bat for this yet.
  - Competition: assumed — public info and the failed prior initiative suggest an aging on-prem warehouse plus a parallel evaluation of at least one other cloud data platform is likely, though not confirmed by the account.
  - Decision Process / Paper Process: assumed heavy, per company size and public-company status — not yet confirmed.

## Anticipated objections

### "We tried something like this two years ago. It went over budget and never delivered what was promised."

- **What it's really testing:** Identify Pain / Decision Criteria — this is a credibility test before anything else gets discussed.
- **The trap:** Reassuring generically ("we're different, our customers love us") without engaging with what actually went wrong last time.
- **Recommended move:** Negative reverse selling — "That's a fair thing to be cautious about. What actually went wrong last time — was it the platform itself, the migration, or how success was measured?" Let them define the real failure mode before proposing anything.

### "How is Snowflake actually different from what we already have, or from Databricks, which I know we're also looking at?"

- **What it's really testing:** Decision Criteria / Competition.
- **The trap:** A feature-by-feature comparison that turns into a spec war the seller can't fully control.
- **Recommended move:** Command of the Message — redirect to the specific cost this VP is already carrying: "Rather than compare feature lists, can I ask what your data engineering team is spending the most hours firefighting on today? That's usually where the real gap between platforms shows up."

### "I'll need to see this justified against other priorities — budget's tight this cycle."

- **What it's really testing:** Metrics / Economic Buyer.
- **The trap:** A generic industry ROI stat not tied to anything Delta-specific.
- **Recommended move:** Compelling event / cost of inaction — anchor to a real, specific cost already being paid (e.g. current warehouse licensing renewal timing, or engineering hours spent on maintenance) rather than an industry-wide average.

### "Your champion on my team is enthusiastic, but I haven't heard this raised by anyone more senior."

- **What it's really testing:** Champion — this is the VP testing whether the deal has real internal traction or just one enthusiastic individual contributor.
- **The trap:** Overselling the champion's influence rather than acknowledging the gap.
- **Recommended move:** Command of the Message — ask directly what it would take for this to get raised at the level it needs to: "Fair — what would need to be true for this to be something your team is actively pushing for, not just supportive of?"

### "What happens if this becomes another project that stalls out in security or procurement review?"

- **What it's really testing:** Paper Process / Decision Process.
- **The trap:** Promising a fast review the seller doesn't control.
- **Recommended move:** Compelling event / cost of inaction, paired with honesty — don't promise speed; instead ask what caused the prior initiative's review to stall, and offer to get ahead of that specific blocker early rather than waiting for it to resurface.

### "Convince me this isn't just a repeat of last time with a different vendor name on it."

- **What it's really testing:** Compound — Identify Pain and Decision Criteria together; this is the escalation objection if earlier answers landed well.
- **The trap:** A confident pitch that doesn't reference anything specific about what actually failed before.
- **Recommended move:** Negative reverse selling into Command of the Message — "Honestly, if the only thing that changed was the vendor name, you'd be right to say no. What would you need to see differently this time to trust it's not that?"
