# Objection Sparring Scorecard — Delta Air Lines

**Persona faced:** VP of Data & Analytics Engineering (economic buyer, skeptical after a prior failed initiative)
**Deal stage:** Evaluation
**Session date:** 2026-08-17

## What worked

- On the "we tried this before" objection, the seller asked what specifically went wrong (migration vs. platform vs. success criteria) instead of reassuring generically — this is negative reverse selling done correctly, and it's what got the VP to volunteer real detail (a checkpoint-based timeline, a pre-agreed definition of success) instead of staying guarded.
- After a weak first answer on the Databricks/differentiation question, the seller recovered by redirecting to a specific, quantifiable cost ("what's your team spending the most hours firefighting on") — a real Command of the Message pivot — and it surfaced a concrete number (two engineers' worth of pipeline-maintenance time) the seller can now build a business case around.

## What to improve

- The first response to "how is this different from Databricks" — "better performance benchmarks and a cleaner UI, you'd like it more once you see it" — is a feature-level defense with no connection to anything the VP has said they care about. It cost the seller ground; the VP's follow-up ("everyone says that") shows the pushback landed. A stronger opening move would have gone straight to the firefighting-hours question rather than needing a second attempt to get there.

## Force Management technique usage

- **Command of the Message:** Used well, on the second attempt at the differentiation question — the firefighting-hours redirect. First attempt was a miss (feature-level answer).
- **Negative reverse selling:** Used well and early, on the "we tried this before" objection — agreeing with the caution instead of arguing past it is what opened the VP up.
- **Compelling event / cost of inaction:** Not yet tested in this excerpt — the budget-justification objection from the battlecard wasn't reached this session.

## Toughest objection this session

The differentiation-vs-Databricks question. Not fully resolved — the seller recovered the tone of the exchange with a strong pivot, but never actually answered what makes Snowflake the better choice for Delta specifically. That's still open and needs a real answer before the next real conversation.

## Suggested next-step talk track

Come back to the VP with a rough estimate of what 2 FTEs of pipeline-maintenance time actually costs annually, and pair it with one specific, concrete reason Snowflake would reduce that (not a feature list) — that directly continues the thread this session opened rather than starting over.
