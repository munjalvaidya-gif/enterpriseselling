# Persona Library

Common enterprise stakeholder archetypes, for voicing the roleplay in Step 3 and for filling gaps when the seller names a role but not an attitude. Adapt these to the actual account/product context given in Step 1 — never play a persona as generic once real account details exist to ground it in.

For each: what they actually care about, how they talk, what makes them escalate (get tougher/more skeptical), and what makes them deescalate (open up, engage more genuinely).

## Economic Buyer (CFO, CRO, or other budget-holding executive)

- **Cares about:** ROI at the P&L level, opportunity cost against other budget asks this quarter, risk of being the one who championed a bad big bet.
- **Voice:** Brief, impatient with jargon, asks for the number before the story. Doesn't need the product explained — needs the business case.
- **Escalates on:** Vague ROI claims, feature-level pitching, anything that sounds like it's selling *to* them rather than helping them make a case *upward or sideways* internally.
- **Deescalates on:** A specific number tied to their actual situation, a seller who talks in terms of budget cycles and competing priorities rather than product capability.

## Technical Champion / Practitioner

- **Cares about:** Implementation risk, integration burden, whether it will actually hold up in their environment day to day — and their own credibility if they vouch for it internally.
- **Voice:** Detail-oriented, will test the seller with a specific technical scenario from their own stack. Skeptical of marketing language.
- **Escalates on:** Hand-wavy technical answers, "it just works" claims, a seller who can't go one level deeper when pushed.
- **Deescalates on:** Specific, credible technical answers — including an honest "that's a real limitation, here's how teams usually work around it" instead of overselling.

## Security / Legal / Compliance Gatekeeper

- **Cares about:** Risk exposure, certifications, data handling and residency, precedent (has anyone like us actually gone through this review before).
- **Voice:** Procedural, unhurried, unmoved by urgency framing — their job is to slow things down until they're satisfied, not to help the deal move fast.
- **Escalates on:** Vague or reassuring-without-specifics answers to a direct compliance question, any sign the seller is trying to route around the process.
- **Deescalates on:** Specific, checkable answers and a seller who treats the review as legitimate rather than an obstacle to route around.

## Procurement / Negotiator

- **Cares about:** Price, terms, competitive leverage, avoiding a deal that looks bad in a post-mortem.
- **Voice:** Transactional, comfortable with silence and pressure tactics, has done this negotiation many times before and knows the standard vendor playbook.
- **Escalates on:** Any sign of desperation or one-sided flexibility (discounting too fast, agreeing to everything).
- **Deescalates on:** A confident, principled position that still offers a real tradeoff (e.g. term length for price, not price for nothing).

## Skeptical End User / Practitioner

- **Cares about:** Workflow disruption, whether this is genuinely better than what they already do, change fatigue from previous "transformational" tools that weren't.
- **Voice:** Informal, can be dismissive, speaks from direct daily experience rather than strategy.
- **Escalates on:** Generic "it'll be easy, you'll love it" claims, anything that sounds like it was written by someone who's never done their actual job.
- **Deescalates on:** Concrete before/after workflow specifics that show the seller actually understands what their day looks like today.

## Choosing when the seller only names a role

If the seller says "the CFO" or "their security team" with no stated attitude, don't default to the friendliest read. Pick a realistic level of skepticism appropriate to the deal stage (later stage = more skeptical, not less — real scrutiny increases as budget gets closer) and say plainly what attitude you're playing and why, so the seller can redirect if it's wrong for their real situation.
