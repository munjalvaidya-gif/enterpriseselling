# MEDDPICC Primer — Objection Edition

MEDDPICC (the extended form of MEDDIC used in Force Management's methodology) is eight questions a rep must be able to answer before they trust a deal is real. In account planning, MEDDPICC is a research checklist. Here, it's a diagnostic lens: **most objections are a surface expression of one specific MEDDPICC gap, not a standalone complaint.** A seller who answers the surface objection without addressing the gap underneath will win the exchange and still lose the deal.

| Letter | Stands for | The real question | What an objection about this actually sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M** | Metrics | What quantifiable outcome does this purchase need to move? | "I'm not sure this moves the needle enough" / "how do we even measure success here" |
| **E** | Economic Buyer | Who has real authority and budget to say yes? | "I'll need to check with my team" / "that's not really my call" / evasiveness about who signs |
| **D** | Decision Criteria | What will this org actually evaluate vendors on? | "We're also comparing this against X" / "price isn't the only factor for us" |
| **D** | Decision Process | What steps and approvals does a purchase like this go through? | "This isn't something we can move quickly on" / "there's a process we have to follow" |
| **P** | Paper Process | What will legal/security/procurement review look like? | "Our legal team will need to look at this" / "security review usually takes a while here" |
| **I** | Identify Pain | What specific, provable pain does this solve? | "Honestly, we're getting by fine right now" / "not sure this is a priority" |
| **C** | Champion | Who inside the org benefits personally and has influence to push it? | "I'd need buy-in from my team first" / a stakeholder who won't commit to advocating internally |
| **C** | Competition | Who else is this account evaluating — incumbent, status quo, a direct competitor? | "We're already using something for this" / "how are you different from [X]" |

## How to use this while building objections

When Step 2 of the skill selects objections for the battlecard, work backward from the MEDDPICC gaps established in Step 1, not forward from a generic objection list:

- **Economic Buyer unconfirmed** → the persona should be vague or noncommittal about approval authority, not name a clear signer. This forces the seller to actually ask, rather than assume.
- **Champion untested** → the persona shouldn't offer to advocate internally unprompted; if the seller never asks for that commitment, the roleplay shouldn't hand it to them for free.
- **Competition unknown** → work in a comparison to an unnamed alternative ("the other option we're looking at") rather than naming a specific competitor the seller hasn't mentioned — don't invent competitor names the seller didn't supply.
- **Decision Process / Paper Process assumed, not confirmed** → these are almost never volunteered by a real buyer; the persona should only surface them if the seller asks, and should be realistically vague on timeline ("depends," "a few weeks, maybe longer") rather than giving a clean answer.

## What "assumed" means here

Unlike account planning, where MEDDPICC gaps get filled by public research, here they can only be filled by what the seller has actually confirmed with the real account. Where Step 1 didn't get a clear answer, mark the element as **assumed** in both the battlecard and the scorecard, and default to the least favorable realistic assumption for that deal stage (see the skill's Step 1) rather than a convenient one — the point of the exercise is to expose real gaps, not paper over them.
